What it does

Project the future value of a lump sum or recurring contribution under compound interest, optionally shown in today’s purchasing power.

How it works

FV = P(1+r)^n + PMT·(((1+r)^n − 1)/r), where r is the periodic rate and n the number of periods. Inflation-adjusted (real) value = FV ÷ (1+inflation)^years.

Example

$10,000 at 7% for 20 years, no extra contributions
$10,000 grows to about $38,697 before inflation.

Questions people ask

What does “compounding frequency” mean?
It is how often interest is added to your balance. More frequent compounding (e.g. daily vs yearly) produces slightly more growth over time.
Why show inflation-adjusted value?
A dollar in 20 years buys less than today. The inflation-adjusted figure tells you what your future balance is really worth in today’s money.
