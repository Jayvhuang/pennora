Frequently asked questions

How does compound interest work?
Compound interest means you earn returns not only on your original money but also on the returns it has already earned. The more frequently interest compounds and the longer you wait, the faster your balance grows.
How is my take-home pay calculated?
Start with gross pay, subtract pre-tax 401(k) and health premiums, then withhold federal income tax using marginal brackets, state income tax, and FICA (Social Security 6.2% plus Medicare 1.45%, with an extra 0.9% above $200,000). What remains is your net pay.
How big should my emergency fund be?
A common rule is three to six months of essential expenses. If your income is unstable or you have dependents, aim for six to nine months.
What is a FIRE number?
Your FIRE number is the invested portfolio size that lets you cover living expenses from returns. A common estimate divides annual spending by a safe withdrawal rate, often 4%, so the target is about 25 times your yearly spending.
Is my data uploaded or stored?
No. All calculations run locally in your browser. Pennora does not collect, upload, or sell any financial information you enter.
Why show inflation-adjusted values?
A dollar today will not buy the same in 20 years. Our compound and FIRE calculators can show results in today's purchasing power so you see what your money is really worth, not just the nominal number.
